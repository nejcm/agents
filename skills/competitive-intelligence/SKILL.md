# Competitive Intelligence

Produce actionable competitive intelligence from ethical, legal, public
sources. The goal is to explain what matters strategically, not to collect the
largest possible list of facts.

## Workflow

1. Define the market, customer segment, geography, timeframe, and the decision
   the analysis must support.
2. Classify direct competitors, indirect competitors, substitutes, adjacent
   products, and plausible entrants. Explain inclusion and exclusion criteria.
3. Gather evidence from product documentation, pricing pages, filings,
   technical material, customer reviews, job postings, announcements, and
   other credible public sources.
4. Benchmark only dimensions relevant to the decision: target customer,
   problem solved, capabilities, packaging, pricing, distribution, proof,
   retention signals, and operational or technical differentiators.
5. Separate confirmed facts from estimates and inference. Validate material
   claims with multiple independent sources where possible.
6. Infer strategic intent cautiously from repeated investments, launches,
   hiring, partnerships, pricing changes, and omissions. State the evidence
   supporting each inference.
7. Rank threats and opportunities by impact, likelihood, confidence, and time
   horizon. Recommend what to do and what to monitor.

## Rules

- Use only ethical, legal intelligence gathering from public sources.
- Cite claims and record access or publication dates for volatile information.
- Do not present marketing claims, estimated market share, or inferred
  capabilities as verified facts.
- Call out missing data, incomparable packages, regional differences, and
  stale evidence.
- Avoid exhaustive tables that do not change a decision.

## Deliverable

Use the smallest useful report, usually:

- Executive conclusion
- Competitive landscape and inclusion criteria
- Evidence-backed benchmark
- Positioning and differentiation analysis
- Threats, opportunities, and strategic implications
- Monitoring signals and recommended cadence
- Sources and confidence notes

Use SWOT or a positioning map only when it clarifies a real comparison.
