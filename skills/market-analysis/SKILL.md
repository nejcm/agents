# Market Analysis

Build a decision-useful view of a market. Quantify where the evidence supports
it, expose assumptions where it does not, and keep the current state separate
from projections.

## Workflow

1. Define the product, customer, geography, timeframe, use case, and decision.
   Clarify whether the question concerns revenue, users, transactions,
   spend, capacity, or another market unit.
2. Map the value chain, buying process, alternatives, major suppliers, and
   relevant regulatory or economic forces.
3. Size the opportunity with at least one defensible method. Prefer a
   bottom-up model using customers, penetration, frequency, and price when
   inputs are available; use top-down estimates as a cross-check.
4. Segment customers by needs, behavior, willingness to pay, context, or
   buying process. Do not treat demographics as useful segmentation without a
   causal link to demand.
5. Evaluate demand signals: existing spend, search or usage behavior, customer
   complaints, adoption, retention, willingness to pay, and competitor growth.
6. Analyze trends and scenarios. Distinguish observed change, causal
   explanation, forecast, and speculation.
7. Identify white spaces, entry barriers, distribution constraints, and the
   smallest test that could validate the biggest assumption.

## Quantitative discipline

- Cite every market figure and state its unit, geography, period, and source.
- Show formulas, inputs, ranges, and sensitivity for TAM/SAM/SOM or forecasts.
- Reconcile top-down and bottom-up estimates; explain differences rather than
  averaging them silently.
- Label estimates, reported figures, projections, and scenarios separately.
- Do not claim precision the data cannot support.

## Report structure

1. Executive conclusion
2. Market definition and methodology
3. Size, growth, and assumptions
4. Customer segments and demand evidence
5. Competitive and value-chain context
6. Opportunities, barriers, and risks
7. Recommended validation step and success metric
8. Sources and limitations
